01 / ABOUT ME

A little about me.

I'm Xueyan Cheng, a doctoral researcher in environmental sciences at the EcoClim group at the University of Freiburg, working at the intersection of ecology, hydrology, and climate science.

My PhD is embedded in the International Max Planck Research School for Global Biogeochemical Cycles (IMPRS-gBGC) at the Max Planck Institute for Biogeochemistry in Jena, Germany.

My research begins with a simple curiosity: when climate conditions become extreme, why do plants respond differently? I combine satellite observations, environmental datasets, and statistical modelling to explore patterns across regions and the processes behind them.

Before studying vegetation responses to climate extremes, I worked on a related question in cities: how much can urban greenery help us cope with heat? I like research that connects big-picture patterns to questions we can actually explain.

I'm looking ahead to the next stage of my research career and am interested in postdoctoral or research scientist roles that bring together environmental observations, ecological questions, and quantitative methods.

03 / RESEARCH

Questions I'm exploring.

Different ecosystems, different responses. I am interested in the reasons behind those differences — and what they mean in a changing climate.

CURRENT FOCUS ✳

Panel (a), Cheng et al. (2026) · cropped from published figure.

Vegetation responses to drought and wet extremes

How strongly, how quickly, and under which conditions does vegetation respond to unusual soil moisture? I examine global and regional response patterns using satellite greenness and environmental data.

Atmospheric demand & response speed

I'm especially interested in vapour pressure deficit (VPD): whether its magnitude and rate of change are associated with how quickly vegetation responds to drought.
